# Complete all-gene ortholog atlas

**Figure | Every gene in the qualifying four-organism ortholog comparison.** The atlas uses the boxed, four-column design of the biological main figure for all 320 distinct species/Entrez genes in the complete Task 4 pair catalogue. All 206 direct relationships appear explicitly. It includes 321 GenAge records because one worm gene has two catalogue records/symbols. The four columns remain budding yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*), nematode (*Caenorhabditis elegans*), fruit fly (*Drosophila melanogaster*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*). The atlas includes every two-species comparison as well as the 22 candidate networks spanning at least three species. “All genes” refers to the complete qualifying ortholog-pair set, not all 2,138 GenAge records in these organisms, many of which lack a qualifying recovered longevity pair.

**Reading the cards.** Each card contains a gene symbol, GenAge record ID(s), an evidence label and either a reviewed experiment or the original catalogue annotation. The 20 **Reviewed example** cards preserve the previously checked gene, intervention, lifespan outcome and primary PMID. Upward green and downward brown arrows refer only to that stated experiment. Brown text also identifies loss of a longevity benefit in a specified mutant background. The 300 **GenAge annotation** cards show the original longevity-influence and lifespan-effect fields, together with every catalogue-linked PMID in the existing reference mapping. They have neutral text and no experiment arrows. These annotations do not identify a specific intervention; for example, Anti-Longevity with a lifespan Increase label must not be read as evidence that increasing gene activity lengthens life. The underlying catalogue may combine deletion, knockdown, overexpression or other experiments. No intervention is invented for an unreviewed card.

“Refs” means a catalogue-linked PubMed ID, not a claim of new primary-paper validation. These references come from the existing frozen Task 3 GenAge/LibAge reference mapping; the original source URLs, hashes and retrieval timestamps are retained in the atlas reference table. The checked 20 experiments retain their exact primary citations, review depths and qualifications in the atlas card table. Where catalogue annotations and a selected experiment differ, both remain available in the source table. The [expanded biological legend](expanded_figure_legend.md) gives the detailed experiment interpretation and original primary links.

**Reading the rows and direct-pair strips.** A row is a connected candidate network used to arrange genes together. The 133 row IDs retain the existing candidate-screen IDs. Neither connectivity nor sharing a row establishes that all genes are pairwise orthologous, that the row is an OMA-defined orthogroup, or that it represents a single conserved mechanism. Every source pair is written below its row using local gene labels: Y1/Y2 for yeast, W1/W2 for worm, F1/F2 for fly and M1/M2 for mouse. Numbering resets within each row. Multiple genes within one organism occupy separate stacked cards, preserving paralogs and many-to-one relationships without choosing one representative. The bracketed relationship codes are copied in the original OMA pair orientation: 1:1 is one-to-one; codes involving n or m denote multiplicity on that side. No pair is inferred through a third gene. A blank species column means that this candidate network contains no gene from that species in the qualifying input set; it does not prove absence of a biological ortholog or lifespan effect.

**Experiment-specific caveats.** Row notes retain female-specific mouse S6K1 and original Igf1r results; the later Igf1r non-replication; the worm-strain scope of the 2011 S6K1 correction; yeast TOR non-one-to-one relationships; the AMPK replicative/animal distinction; the eat-2 background for worm ATG7; and the sch9Δ and connective-tissue contexts for yeast and mouse SOD2. TOR and S6K share a pathway. The insulin/IGF row now includes catalogue-only Insr as well as the previously displayed Igf1r, so its complete network has three direct pairs rather than the selected figure's two. The experiments themselves have not changed. DR means dietary restriction; CLS, chronological lifespan; KO, knockout; RNAi, RNA interference.

**Coverage and limits.** The atlas is exhaustive for the frozen 206-pair catalogue; the underlying orthology resource and mapping have limits. The all-nine audit remains supporting provenance, and Podospora and hamster lack OMA source representation. The atlas is descriptive: it contains no new statistical tests, pooled effects, estimates of mechanistic conservation or new primary-paper review for the remaining 300 genes. A database survival association can reflect developmental or general-fitness effects. Yeast replicative lifespan, chronological survival and animal lifespan are different endpoints.

**Files and reproduction.** The combined `figures/all_gene_atlas/all_gene_ortholog_atlas.pdf` contains all pages with bookmarks. Every page also has PNG, editable SVG, PDF and 600-dpi TIFF exports at 183 × 247 mm. The local `index.html` lets readers find genes, species, GenAge IDs or row IDs and follow the page links. `results/all_gene_atlas/gene_cards.csv` locates every gene; `all_displayed_pairs.csv` locates every exact relationship; `page_index.csv` lists all rows; `catalogue_references.csv` preserves references and provenance. The summary records source hashes. Run `scripts/build_all_gene_atlas.py`, then `scripts/verify_all_gene_atlas.py`. The verifier independently reconstructs all 320 gene keys and all 206 source pairs, reconciles every page's actual SVG/PDF card text, checks all exports and physical dimensions, and checks source integrity. The builder measures every text element against its assigned box and checks overlap. No shared raw source file is modified.
