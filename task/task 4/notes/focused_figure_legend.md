# Focused Task 4 figure — review legend

**Main claim.** Selected, explicitly supported ortholog relationships connect model genes with lifespan effects in the principal GenAge organisms. The figure illustrates recurrence of longevity association, not a count of independent conserved genes or proof of identical molecular effects.

**Figure.** Columns show budding yeast (*S. cerevisiae*), nematode (*C. elegans*), fruit fly (*D. melanogaster*) and mouse (*M. musculus*); rows are three illustrative signalling examples, not source-defined orthogroups. Each occupied cell names the GenAge Build 21 model gene, an intervention, the reported direction of the lifespan outcome and a primary-paper PMID. TOR has six explicit OMA All.May2026 pairs among the four shown genes, but its three yeast-related pairs are one-to-many. S6 kinase has worm–fly and fly–mouse pairs; no direct worm–mouse edge was recovered. For the insulin/IGF receptor example, the displayed pairs are worm *daf-2*–fly *InR* and worm *daf-2*–mouse *Igf1r*. The latter is a one-to-many relation because mouse *Insr* is another OMA-linked paralog. A direct fly–mouse pair was not recovered in the frozen analysis. No missing edge is filled by transitivity.

**What the experiments say.** Reduced TOR-related activity accompanies longer life in four distinct assays, including yeast replicative lifespan and mouse median survival. Reduced S6K activity accompanies longer life in worm and fly, and a female-specific increase in mice; the male S6K1-knockout effect was not significant. Reduced insulin/IGF-receptor signalling accompanies longer life in worm and fly, while the displayed female mouse *Igf1r* result comes from the original cohort and was **not reproduced in a later large-cohort study** ([PMID 22132081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22132081/)); genetic-background dependence has been proposed in a subsequent primary study ([PMID 23898955](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23898955/)). These involve different receptor family members and endocrine settings. TOR and S6K share a signalling pathway, and the fly TOR and S6K cells cite the same experimental paper, so they are not independent demonstrations of two conserved mechanisms.

**Interpretation limits.** GenAge's Anti-Longevity label is catalogue-level compression; it is not a directly comparable intervention or effect size. Yeast replicative lifespan, adult invertebrate lifespan and mouse survival are different endpoints. The examples are deliberately selected, so the figure cannot estimate prevalence among all longevity genes. Empty selection cells do not assert absence of orthology, GenAge evidence or biological effect. OMA represents seven of nine Build 21 species; the all-nine inventory and unsupported organisms remain in the [refined source audit](../results/oma_pairwise/refined/README.md). Paper checks are mostly primary abstract/record-level, with targeted mouse S6K1 full-text review, not a systematic quality-of-evidence assessment. The [2011 S6K1 correction](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=f39eea152043b5246fdfa4632451e33eaa76436f&repid=rep1&type=pdf) reports that a worm *rsks-1;aak-2* strain lacked the *rsks-1* allele. New epistasis experiments confirmed AMPK dependence of the *rsks-1* lifespan effect but changed the interpretation of growth and reproduction effects; the correction does not report a change to mouse survival. The mouse *Igf1r* replication conflict means this row should be treated as a cautionary example, not a definitive three-species demonstration. Do not write “the longevity function is evolutionarily conserved” without deeper, comparable intervention review.

**Selection and source data.** These three examples were chosen because they are interpretable for a review and have primary lifespan interventions traceable in the major organisms; they are not the top three by an automated score. The ten displayed GenAge records and intervention summaries are in [focused_experiment_evidence.tsv](focused_experiment_evidence.tsv); the ten exact selected OMA edges are in [selected_oma_pairs.csv](../results/focused/selected_oma_pairs.csv). GenAge IDs, pair cardinalities and annotations are copied from the frozen local Build 21/OMA analysis; references were manually checked against the linked primary records below. The broader 228-pair table and nine-species gap ledger are preserved, not promoted to the main review figure.

**Primary papers:** TOR yeast [PMID 16293764](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16293764/), worm [14668850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14668850/), fly [15186745](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15186745/), mouse [23994476](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23994476/); S6K worm [17266680](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17266680/), fly [15186745](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15186745/), mouse [19797661](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19797661/); insulin/IGF receptor worm [8247153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8247153/), fly [11292875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11292875/), mouse [12483226](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12483226/).

**Proposed one-sentence review takeaway:** “GenAge and explicit model-to-model orthology highlight recurrent lifespan effects in TOR/S6K and insulin/IGF receptor signalling across major experimental organisms, while paralogy, intervention type, sex and lifespan endpoint constrain any claim of conserved causal function.”
